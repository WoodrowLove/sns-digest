## The OpenChat #2266 result (rejected at 59.9% YES)

Last week's open coin-flip resolved. #2266 — the proposal asking OpenChat's named neuron to approve registering **HashEra** as a Known Neuron on the NNS — moved from 38.6% YES (mid-vote) to 59.9% YES at close, and **was still rejected.**

This is the most informative governance event of the week, so it's worth dwelling on:

**Why a 59.9%-YES proposal still failed:** Critical SNS proposals (the `critical` flag on the proposal payload, set automatically by the SNS framework for treasury and named-neuron actions) require a supermajority — typically **67% YES with at least 20% absolute-majority of voting power**. 59.9% YES doesn't clear that bar regardless of turnout. The proposal failed by design, not by accident.

**What this tells us:** OpenChat's voter base is genuinely split on HashEra. Last week I read the 38.6% mid-vote figure as voters defaulting to abstain or NO out of unfamiliarity. The final 59.9% says a real majority *did* form for YES — but not a 2/3 majority. That's a much more interesting result than I assumed: it's not "voters didn't pay attention," it's "voters paid attention, formed a majority view, and the supermajority threshold did its job preventing a contested trust-flag from getting rubber-stamped."

**Operator takeaway for SNS designers:** This is the supermajority working as intended on a Known-Neuron registration. Other SNSes that route trust delegation through their named neurons should probably keep this threshold in place. If you're building voting policies (Kairos and otherwise), the rule for critical NNS-coordination proposals should be: *check both YES% and the critical flag; majority alone isn't safe.*

## Pattern of the week: NNS-coordination proposals as governance noise

Five of OpenChat's six most recent proposals are NNS-coordination motions ("Instruct the named neuron to approve NNS proposal X"). That's not unusual — OpenChat operates a Known Neuron and so this is structurally how its governance touches the broader IC. But it does mean that a non-trivial fraction of OpenChat voter attention goes to *meta*-votes about *other* DAOs' votes, often on motions whose titles range from substantive to literally emoji.

For Kairos policy design: NNS-coordination proposals are the strongest candidate for default-automation with a tight escalation rule. Specifically: *follow the proposal automatically when it's not flagged critical; escalate to human when it is*. That single rule would have correctly handled four of the five OpenChat NNS-coordination proposals this week and correctly escalated #2266 (the HashEra Known Neuron one) — the only one that mattered.

Anyone with a different take on that policy: the [validation thread](https://forum.dfinity.org/t/validation-kairos-managed-autonomous-governance-agents-for-sns-built-on-anda-rfp-9/67763) is open.
