**How I'd read it:** If you don't have a view on HashEra, the right move is abstain, not follow. The OpenChat community normally runs 90%+ on these routine NNS-coordination proposals. The 38.6% here is signal, not noise.

## The KongSwap story (rejected 2.6% YES)

Proposal #363, *"Save the KongSwap DAO by Transferring SNS Management to Doxa Foundation"*, was rejected this week with 2.6% YES.

**Background:** KongSwap team announced they're sunsetting the project and instructed liquidity providers to withdraw by April 6. The Doxa Foundation publicly offered to [take over and keep running it](https://forum.dfinity.org/t/doxa-foundation-official-statement-kongswap-sunset/65980).

**What the rejection means:** The community did not want a foundation-led takeover. At 2.6% YES, this wasn't a close call — it's a clear "no." Whether that reflects distrust of Doxa specifically, preference for an orderly wind-down, or simply a fragmented voter base that couldn't mobilize is unclear without more data.

**Operator takeaway:** Sunset governance is hard. There's no standard pattern on ICP for what happens to an SNS's treasury and tokens when the team walks away. Worth a forum discussion.

## The ORIGYN rejections (five in a row, all ~4% YES)

ORIGYN has a cluster of token-economic proposals that all failed hard:

| # | Title | YES |
|---|---|---|
| 142 | Increase Reward Rate to 10% | 4.5% |
| 143 | Increase transaction fee from 0.002 to 1 token | 4.3% |
| 144 | Increase transaction fee to 2 tokens | 4.1% |
| 145 | New OGY Reward Parameters | 3.9% |
| 146 | Decrease Max Dissolve Delay | 4.0% |

**What's happening:** Either (a) a single proposer is repeatedly attempting unpopular parameter changes without community alignment, (b) whale voters systematically reject any parameter motion, or (c) most voters aren't paying attention and the handful who do vote NO.

Looking at the uniform 4% YES across very different proposal types, explanation (b) is most likely — ORIGYN's vote is concentrated enough that one big holder's NO vote crushes every change proposal regardless of merit. That's a governance health signal worth noting.

**Operator takeaway:** If you hold OGY and care about any of these parameters, you need to coordinate with other holders before submission, not after. Five rejections and ~500 ICP in reject fees burned is an expensive lesson.

It got **62.8% YES but was still rejected** — meaning it needed a supermajority that it couldn't reach.

**Read:** ~37% of ELNA's voting weight said no to paid X shilling from the DAO treasury. That's a meaningful cultural line — even with real performance stats and marketing urgency, treasury-funded shilling didn't clear the bar. Worth remembering when people say "DAOs don't have values."
